Part 0

A body you cannot see on an X-ray

By now you know what yoga is (S1), how the eight limbs structure the path (S2), and the paths of the Gita (S3). Today we cross into the part of yoga that trips up newcomers and fascinates everyone who stays: the subtle body - the energetic map of sheaths, channels, centers and winds that the tradition uses to describe your inner life. None of it shows up on a scan. All of it has shaped how yoga is taught for centuries, and it is a graded topic in your training's subtle-anatomy strand.

How to hold this material honestly These are experiential and traditional maps, not clinical anatomy. When you teach them, use the language "traditionally said to" - a chakra is "traditionally said to" govern a theme; a vayu is "traditionally said to" move in a direction. You are passing on a rich, centuries-old system of self-understanding, taught as tradition rather than as medical fact - and it is still deeply useful for helping students feel and name their inner experience.
Part 1 · covers the five sheaths of being

The five koshas 5 min live

The oldest map of the subtle body comes from the Taittiriya Upanishad: the self is wrapped in five koshas - sheaths, or layers - from the dense physical body on the outside to the innermost layer of bliss. Think of them as nested shells: to reach the center you move inward through each one. Yoga practice works from the outside in, which is why we start with the physical body and end in stillness.

LiveThe five sheaths, from flesh to bliss2.5 min▶

The koshas give a teacher a simple, humane model: a person is more than a body, and a good practice touches every layer. When you cue a physical pose (annamaya), guide the breath (pranamaya), settle the mind (manomaya), invite awareness (vijnanamaya) and let a student rest in ease (anandamaya), you are moving through all five without naming them.

KoshaEnglishWhat it is
AnnamayaFood / physicalThe gross body of flesh and bone - the outermost sheath, nourished by food
PranamayaEnergy / breathThe life-force body; breath and prana that animate the physical
ManomayaMental-emotionalThe mind of thoughts, feelings and the processing of the senses
VijnanamayaWisdom / intellectDiscernment, insight, the inner witness that observes the mind
AnandamayaBlissThe innermost sheath - pure joy and being, closest to the true self
Real world

Why "just breathe" is not shallow advice. When a stressed student can barely move, you do not start at the wisdom layer. You meet them at the sheath that is available - often the breath (pranamaya) - and let the calming ripple inward to the mind. The kosha map is really a teaching order: reach the layer the student can actually feel today.

Self-studyWhere the koshas come from1 min read▶

The five-sheath model appears in the Taittiriya Upanishad, one of the older Upanishads you met on the Session 1 timeline. The Upanishads are where yoga turns inward - away from ritual and toward the nature of the self - so it is fitting that our first map of the inner body is theirs. Memorize the order outer to inner; it is the spine of every later subtle-body idea.

Part 2 · covers the three qualities + the life-force winds

Gunas & prana 5 min live

Two ideas here. First the gunas - three qualities that, in the Samkhya philosophy underlying yoga, make up all of nature and every state of mind. Then prana, the life-force itself, which the tradition says moves through the body as five vayus or "winds", each with its own direction and job.

LivePrana and the five vayus2.5 min▶

Prana is the life-force - the same word gives us pranayama (Session 9). The tradition describes it flowing through the body as five vayus, or winds, each moving in a characteristic direction and governing a bodily function. You do not need to feel these to teach them; you need to know the names, seats and directions.

VayuSeat / locationTraditionally governs
PranaChest / heartIntake, inhalation - the inward, receiving current
ApanaPelvis / lower abdomenElimination, the downward-moving current
SamanaNavelDigestion and assimilation - the balancing, inward current
UdanaThroat / headThe upward current: speech, exhalation, uplift
VyanaWhole bodyCirculation - the pervading current through every limb
Real world

The arc of a good practice is rajas to sattva. A class often begins a little tamasic (heavy, unmotivated bodies on mats), builds through rajasic effort (standing poses, sun salutations, heat and drive), then settles into sattvic clarity by Savasana. When students say a class "cleared their head", they are describing a shift from rajas into sattva. You will design that arc deliberately in Session 7.

Self-studyGunas beyond the mat1 min read▶

The gunas were introduced philosophically in Session 1 as part of Samkhya, yoga's parent system. Traditionally they describe not only states of mind but qualities of food, environments and actions - a heavy meal is tamasic, a stimulating one rajasic, a fresh light one sattvic. Use the idea lightly and honestly: it is a lens for noticing your own state, not a rigid science.

Part 3 · covers the seven chakras + the nadis

The seven chakras 6 min live

The chakras are the most famous - and most misunderstood - piece of the subtle body. Traditionally they are energy centers arranged along the central channel of the spine, from the base (muladhara) to the crown (sahasrara). Each is associated with a location, and the lower ones with an element and a life-theme. Learn them bottom to top, the direction energy is said to rise.

An honest note on the elements Only the lower five chakras map cleanly to the gross elements (earth, water, fire, air, ether) in classical tantra. For ajna and sahasrara, sources genuinely disagree - many describe them as "beyond the elements", while popular modern charts assign light or thought. Teach the lower five as canonical and the top two as "beyond the elements, varies by source". That precision is what a well-read teacher does.
LiveThe nadis: three energy channels1.5 min▶

The chakras are said to sit along nadis - subtle channels through which prana flows. Three matter for your training:

  • Ida - runs on the left; cooling, lunar, calming.
  • Pingala - runs on the right; heating, solar, activating.
  • Sushumna - the central channel along the spine; the path through which kundalini energy is traditionally said to rise, from root to crown.

This is why hatha means sun (ha) and moon (tha): the practice is said to balance pingala and ida so energy can rise through the central sushumna. Nadi shodhana, the alternate-nostril breath you meet in Session 9, is literally "channel cleansing".

Part 4 · covers the energy locks + the Ayurvedic doshas

Bandhas & the doshas 5 min live

Two last pieces. The bandhas are energetic "locks" - gentle internal engagements said to contain and direct prana. Then Ayurveda, yoga's sister science, which reads the whole body-mind through three doshas or constitutions. Both come back in the physical anatomy of Session 5.

LiveThe doshas: Ayurveda's three constitutions2 min▶

Ayurveda is the traditional Indian science of life and yoga's sister discipline. It reads every body-mind as a blend of three doshas, each built from a pair of elements. Most people have one or two that dominate. You are not diagnosing anyone - but knowing the doshas helps you meet different students where they are.

DoshaElementsTraditionally governs
VataSpace + airMovement, the nervous system, lightness and change
PittaFire + waterDigestion, metabolism, heat, drive and focus
KaphaWater + earthStructure, stability, steadiness and calm
✦ Reflect nowWhich dosha reads as dominant in you today - airy and restless (vata), fiery and driven (pitta), or steady and heavy (kapha)? And which guna from Part 2 - sattva, rajas or tamas - matches your mind right now? Notice how the two maps rhyme.
Self-studyWhy bandhas are taught gently1 min read▶

Traditionally the bandhas contain prana and direct it into the central channel. Physically - as you will see in Session 5 - mula bandha engages the pelvic floor and uddiyana the deep abdominal muscles, so they double as core support. In a modern class they are introduced subtly, as a light lift rather than a forceful grip, and jalandhara mostly appears in seated breathwork. Never teach them as strain.

Reflect & apply 1 of 1

Locate the map on your own body ✦ 8 min · everyone does this

The subtle body is best learned by feeling for it, not just reading it. This is the build-along of a subtle-anatomy session: not a pose, but a guided inner tour. Do it seated and quiet.

Find the three bandhas. Gently lift the pelvic floor (mula), then lightly draw the lower belly up and in (uddiyana), then soften the chin toward the chest (jalandhara). Just locate each - no forcing, no holding the breath.

Notice ida and pingala. Close your eyes and feel which nostril is breathing more freely right now. The tradition maps left to the cooling ida, right to the heating pingala. Whichever is open is a small live reading of your own channels.

Scan the sheaths. Move attention outer to inner: the body (annamaya), the breath (pranamaya), the mind's chatter (manomaya), the quiet witness behind it (vijnanamaya). Rest a moment in whatever ease you find (anandamaya).

Keep this Note which nostril was dominant and which dosha and guna felt strongest today. Doing this over a week teaches you more about the subtle-body maps than any chart - and gives you honest language for how they feel, not just what they mean.
Homework

Before Session 5 ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Memorize the five koshas in order, outer to inner: annamaya → pranamaya → manomaya → vijnanamaya → anandamaya.
  • Memorize the seven chakras bottom to top: muladhara → svadhisthana → manipura → anahata → vishuddha → ajna → sahasrara.
  • Memorize the five vayus (prana, apana, samana, udana, vyana) with their seats, and the three bandhas (mula, uddiyana, jalandhara).
  • Memorize the three gunas (sattva, rajas, tamas) and the three doshas (vata, pitta, kapha) with their elements.
  • For each chakra, write one honest sentence in your own words - and mark ajna and sahasrara as "beyond the elements".

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The outermost of the five koshas - the physical, food body - is...

Annamaya, the "food body", is the outermost sheath; anandamaya (bliss) is the innermost. Practice moves from outer to inner.

2 · Which chakra is located at the throat?

Vishuddha sits at the throat, element ether/space, theme expression. Anahata is the heart; ajna is between the eyebrows.

3 · Kundalini energy is traditionally said to rise through which central channel?

Sushumna is the central channel along the spine; ida (left) and pingala (right) flank it. Hatha balances the two so energy can rise through the center.
