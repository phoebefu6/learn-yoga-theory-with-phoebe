# Research brief 2 - Asana, sequencing, anatomy, teaching (verified)

Correct term is **asana** (Sanskrit asana); "athana" is a phonetic misspelling with no textual basis. ⚠ = sources disagree.

RYS-200 (2020 Elevated Standards): Techniques/Training/Practice 75h · Anatomy & Physiology 30h · Yoga Humanities 30h · Professional Essentials 50h (incl. teaching methodology + practicum) + 15 flex. Anatomy category covers physical AND subtle anatomy + "benefits and cautions."

## 1. Asana theory

### Definition - Sthira Sukham Asanam (Sutra 2.46)
Posture = steady (sthira) + comfortable (sukha). Sthira = stable/grounded strength, not rigidity. Sukha = "good space" (su+kha), easeful. Core principle: **balance of opposing forces** (effort/ease, strength/flexibility) - the philosophical basis for not forcing.

### Categories (what each does)
| Category | Effect |
|---|---|
| Standing | lower-body strength, balance, postural awareness; most activating |
| Seated / forward folds | posterior-chain flexibility, hip mobility; calming; base for pranayama |
| Backbends | energizing, heart/chest-opening, spinal flexibility |
| Twists | circulation, spinal health ⚠ (sources split: energizing vs calming/detoxifying) |
| Inversions | circulation, focus, clarity (thyroid claim = "traditionally said to") |
| Arm balances | upper-body + core strength, focus (usually peak-pose material) |
| Restorative | recovery, integration, parasympathetic calm |
Well-rounded class includes multiple types (complementary directions).

### Alignment principles
Stack the joints · distribute weight evenly · engage the core · build from the foundation/base up (ground contact points) · move from center of gravity · bandhas for energetic engagement.

### Sanskrit naming logic
Almost all names end in **-asana** ("pose/seat"). Names describe appearance/position/inspiration. tada=mountain -> Tadasana. Worked example: **Adho Mukha Svanasana** = adho (down) + mukha (face) + svana (dog) + asana = downward-facing dog. Pattern: direction + body part/orientation + animal/object + asana.

### Contraindications, modifications, props
Props (blocks, straps, bolsters, blankets) bring the floor closer, enable alignment, allow longer holds, keep restricted bodies (injury/stiffness/pregnancy) practicing. Blocks = height/stability; straps = extend reach (not to yank deeper); bolsters = restorative support. Governing rule: **never force into discomfort; honor current capacity.** Injuries come from overexertion, poor alignment, no warm-up, poses attempted prematurely.

### Counterposes (pratikriyasana)
After a deep pose, a counterpose neutralizes tension and returns spine/joints toward neutral. Sits inside vinyasa krama.

### 4 Bandhas
Mula (root lock, pelvic floor - neutral pelvis, stops energy dissipating down) · Uddiyana (upward abdominal lock, engages transverse abdominis) · Jalandhara (throat/chin, mainly in pranayama) · Maha Bandha (all three).

## 2. Sequencing / course-flow theory

### The arc
Centering -> warm-up -> sun salutations -> standing poses -> peak pose -> cooling/floor -> Savasana. Mountain metaphor: gradual ascent (prep) -> summit (peak) -> descent (counterpose + restorative) -> rest. Energetic arc: rajasic build -> sattvic settle (maps to ANS + gunas).

### Vinyasa Krama & peak-pose method
Vinyasa krama = ordered steps to progressively sequence poses (attributed to Krishnamacharya). Peak-pose method: the peak is the most demanding pose; every other pose earns its place by preparing for it. To sequence around a peak: identify the joint actions the peak demands (e.g. hip external rotation, shoulder flexion, spinal extension) -> warm those areas with progressively deeper prep -> peak -> counterpose -> cool. Two rules: **warm relevant joints before the peak; counterpose after deep poses.**

### Sun Salutations (Surya Namaskar)
Both start/end in Tadasana; one breath per movement, classically ujjayi; if breath can't keep up, slow the movement. ⚠ pose/breath counts vary by school - present the sequence, not a fixed number.
**Sun A**: Tadasana -> (inh) Urdhva Hastasana -> (exh) Uttanasana -> (inh) Ardha Uttanasana -> (exh) Chaturanga -> (inh) Urdhva Mukha Svanasana (Up Dog) -> (exh) Adho Mukha Svanasana (Down Dog, ~5 breaths) -> Ardha Uttanasana -> Uttanasana -> Urdhva Hastasana -> Tadasana.
**Sun B**: same as A PLUS Utkatasana (Chair) at open/close and Virabhadrasana I (Warrior I) between Up Dog and Down Dog on each side. Sun A = mobility wave (warms spine/back body); Sun B = strength + asymmetry (unilateral legs, deeper hips).

### Class types
| Style | Flow / holds |
|---|---|
| Vinyasa flow | dynamic, breath-linked, continuous; raises HR |
| Hatha | slower, hold postures, controlled breath, more stretch time |
| Yin | long holds 2-20 min; deep connective tissue; can be intense |
| Restorative | holds 5-20 min, fully prop-supported; pure relaxation |
⚠ Yin (connective-tissue load, intense) vs Restorative (fully supported relaxation) - often confused.

## 3. Functional anatomy (~30h)

### Nervous system ("why yoga calms")
ANS = sympathetic (fight-or-flight) + parasympathetic (rest-and-digest). **Vagus nerve** = longest parasympathetic nerve, ~75% of parasympathetic outflow; runs brainstem->throat->lungs->heart->diaphragm->gut. Breath affects vagal tone; **lengthening the exhale increases parasympathetic tone**. Maps onto the 3 gunas.

### Skeletal / planes
3 planes: sagittal (flexion/extension, forward-back), frontal/coronal (side-to-side), transverse (rotation). Joints = where two bones meet. Flexion = rounding forward; extension = arching back (both sagittal).

### Spine
Regions: cervical (neck, 7), thoracic (upper), lumbar (lower). Neutral spine = natural curves preserved.

### Key muscles
Iliopsoas (psoas major + iliacus) = strongest hip flexor, only muscle tethering legs to spine (lumbar -> pelvis). Hamstrings (over-stretched, under-strengthened in yoga). Spinal erectors (extend spine). Core (broad): transverse abdominis, rectus, obliques, multifidus, erectors, pelvic floor, iliopsoas.

### Common injury areas & safe practice
Wrists (spread fingers, shoulders not past wrists) · lower back (bend knees in folds, engage lower abs; most common complaint) · knees (micro-bend; Warrior II front knee over ankle, never collapse in) · shoulders (Chaturanga: elbows hug ribs, shoulders above elbows) · neck (support in head/shoulder stands, never force).

### Breathing anatomy
Diaphragm = primary breathing muscle + ANS regulator; intercostals assist rib expansion. Three-part breath (Dirga): belly -> ribs -> chest. Parasympathetic-activating breaths: Dirga, ujjayi, nadi shodhana.

## 4. Teaching methodology + analogy theory

### Cueing channels
Verbal cues · demonstration · hands-on assists · pointing to the breath. **3-cue rule**: ~3 cues per pose max (avoid overload).

### ⭐ Analogy / metaphor / imagery theory (priority)
Why imagery beats anatomical cues for most students: the brain leans toward metaphor over textbook facts; the hard part isn't receiving detail, it's making sense of it. Metaphor maps an abstract/unfamiliar concept onto a concrete familiar one - lowers cognitive load. **Mental imagery** directs the student to the sensory/felt experience, giving something concrete to embody. Metaphors are actionable ("we intuitively know what to do") and a recognized didactic tool for hard postures. Canonical images: "root down like tree roots," "lengthen the spine like a string pulling the crown," "soften/melt," limbs "reaching."

### Language - positive & invitational
Invitational cueing ("you might explore...," "see if...," "notice...") over command/authoritarian tone. Offer options, not corrections. Adjustments guide, not force; a student should reach the same benefit without touch.

### Hands-on assists & consent (trauma-informed)
Explicit enthusiastic consent is non-negotiable. Opt-in consent cards/chips; normalize declining; can change mind mid-class. Explain what/why before touching. "The power of choice must always stay with the student."

### Class management / presence
Observe students · hold space · offer modifications · voice, pacing, presence. Teacher facilitates, not commands.
